## Primary motifs for the temporary landing

The six-library set is reduced to four primary motifs.

### 1. M01 — CONTROLLED VARIATION

Use one stable identity system that can produce multiple states without losing recognition. Borrow the rule of constrained parameters, not the reference letterforms, palettes, or brand behavior.

Evidence: Studio Dumbar DEMO; FIELD Scalable Storytelling; FIELD adidas REAL/RAW/FAST.

### 2. M03 — AMBIGUOUS CENTER

Keep one central form or field familiar enough to invite recognition but unresolved enough to resist naming. The center may shift between material, trace, object, and atmosphere.

Evidence: onformative AI Inspiration Tool and the abstract-to-concrete exploration path.

### 3. M04 — ORDER + ONE CONTRADICTION

Build a calm, legible system and introduce one deliberate exception: a displaced label, singular color, interrupted grid, or scale break. The exception must be structural, not decorative noise.

Evidence: Bureau Borsche Bavarian State Opera; Teenage Engineering OP-1 and layout system.

### 4. M06 — ARCHIVE / RETURN / TIME

Treat the landing as a current entry into a living archive. Seasons, returns, editions, records, and elapsed time should be visible as information architecture rather than nostalgia styling.

Evidence: Ffern Archive and Artefacts.

## Supporting motifs, not primary

- M02 DATA BECOMES BEHAVIOR: retained as a future interaction rule. Evidence from FIELD IBM Think 2020 and onformative Growing Data.
- M05 AMBIENT RESIDUE: retained as a future motion/space layer. Evidence from Samsung The Wall. It may support M03 but should not become a separate first-screen effect.

## First-screen recommendation

Use M01 + M03 + M04 as the visual grammar. Use M06 for navigation and record structure. Keep M02 and M05 dormant until a later approved interaction pass.

The center should remain small and controlled, the page structure quiet, and one contradiction clearly authored. Avoid full-screen spectacle, branded reference mimicry, luxury-beige imitation, decorative particles, and generic AI gradients.
